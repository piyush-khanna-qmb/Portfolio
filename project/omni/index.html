The wall every automation app hits

Tasker, IFTTT, Shortcuts, Samsung and Google Routines all share one problem: you have to learn their language before they will learn yours. Visual rule-builders, block diagrams, app-specific recipes. Every one of them is an interface you must be trained on before it becomes useful, which is why most people install one, build a single automation, and never open it again.

Trigger and action, nothing else

Every automation that has ever existed is two primitives: something the operating system can detect, and something the operating system can do. Every tool in this category is just a different interface bolted onto that same pair. So instead of building another interface, enumerate the OS capability exhaustively and point a parser at that enumeration. The catalogue is the product. Not a chatbot wrapper, a harness.

Deterministic first, model second

An on-device parser of roughly 1,100 lines resolves the overwhelming majority of real phrasing instantly, with no network call and no inference cost. A cloud model exists as a fallback for genuinely ambiguous prompts, but the product functions without it, and most usage never reaches it. That ordering matters commercially as much as technically: the unit economics work before a single subscriber exists.

Ask, do not guess

Any ambiguity surfaces as an explicit choice rather than a silent assumption. Which contact, which app, which saved location. That is not politeness, it is the difference between a useful tool and a dangerous one once actions have real-world consequences. For anything irreversible the app stops short on purpose: calls open the dialer pre-filled rather than dialling, messages and emails fill the compose screen rather than sending.

What it looks like to use

No rule-builder and no setup screen. You write the sentence, it resolves what you meant, and asks when it genuinely cannot tell.

The Perfect Morning Routine

“Every morning, start waking me up usually at 7 AM with alarms spaced 10 minutes apart until I'm awake. If I start actively scrolling through my phone or turn on my bedroom ceiling lights, consider me awake and stop the remaining alarms. Then give me a concise morning briefing with the latest news, my commute to work, today's calendar and priorities, and add to my Google Tasks the to-do list based on my conversations and interactions with you yesterday. If traffic is unusually bad, suggest when I should leave.”

  1. “start waking me up usually at 7 AM with alarms spaced 10 minutes apart until I'm awake. If I start actively scrolling through my phone or turn on my bedroom ceiling lights, consider me awake”

    ↳Schedules a repeating ladder of alarms from 07:00, ten minutes apart; either scrolling your phone or the bedroom lights coming on counts as awake and cancels whatever's left.

    AlarmManager.setExactAndAllowWhileIdle + Intent.ACTION_USER_PRESENT + AccessibilityService

  2. “a concise morning briefing with the latest news, my commute, today's calendar, and add to my Google Tasks the to-do list”

    ↳Headlines, live commute time and today's events in one grouped notification, with yesterday's loose ends written to your task list.

    Directions API + CalendarContract.Instances + Google Tasks API + NotificationCompat.InboxStyle

Volume That Follows the Road

“When I'm driving, map my song's volume to my speed. On traffic and red lights, set volume to 10%, and on highway max speed, volume can go up to 80%.”

  1. “When I'm driving, map my song's volume to my speed”

    ↳Picks up driving from sustained speed on its own, then maps current speed onto a volume curve rather than a single on-off rule.

    ActivityRecognition IN_VEHICLE + FusedLocationProviderClient getSpeed()

  2. “On traffic and red lights, set volume to 10%, and on highway max speed, volume can go up to 80%”

    ↳Near-stationary drops the stream to a tenth; the top of the curve is capped at 80% so highway speed never pins it at max.

    AudioManager.setStreamVolume(STREAM_MUSIC), clamped against getStreamMaxVolume()

Arriving at Work, Already Caught Up

“When I reach my office, open Keka and mark my attendance. Then check my work items for today and send me a concise summary on Slack, including my pending tasks, deadlines, meetings, and anything that needs my attention. If I haven't checked those messages, remind me before I get caught up in work.”

  1. “When I reach my office, open Keka and mark my attendance”

    ↳A geofence on the saved office location fires once on entry and launches the attendance app straight to the action.

    GeofencingClient.addGeofences + PendingIntent + getLaunchIntentForPackage deep link

  2. “send me a concise summary on Slack. If I haven't checked those messages, remind me”

    ↳Today's tasks, deadlines and meetings as one digest where you already work, with a single nudge if it goes unread.

    CalendarContract + Tasks API + Slack webhook + NotificationListenerService

A Nighttime Routine That Takes No For an Answer

“Every night after midnight, if I open Instagram, gently suggest that I read my novel instead and offer to open my reading app. If I choose to continue using Instagram, enable my blue-light filter, postpone my morning alarms by 30 minutes, and remind me that staying up late will affect tomorrow's sleep. Don't keep interrupting me after I've made my choice.”

  1. “Every night after midnight, if I open Instagram, gently suggest that I read my novel instead and offer to open my reading app”

    ↳A time window plus that specific app coming to the foreground surfaces one actionable prompt, the reading app a single tap away.

    AccessibilityService TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED + time guard + NotificationCompat.Action

  2. “enable my blue-light filter, postpone my morning alarms by 30 minutes. Don't keep interrupting me”

    ↳If you stay it limits the damage and rewrites tomorrow's alarms, then records the choice so the rule cannot fire again tonight.

    Settings.ACTION_NIGHT_DISPLAY_SETTINGS + AlarmManager reschedule + per-night flag
